# Bayes' Theorem (3Blue1Brown)

## Opening excerpt

> Our goal is for you to come away from this lesson understanding one of the most important formulas in probability, Bayes' theorem. This formula is central to scientific discovery. It's a core tool in machine learning and AI, and it has even been used for treasure hunting! In the 80's a small team led by Tommy Thompson used Bayesian search tactics to help uncover a ship that had sunk a century and half earlier carrying $700,000,000 worth of gold (in today's terms). So it's a formula worth understanding.

## Distinctive excerpt

> Before reading the description of Steve, there was about a 20-to-1 chance that he was a farmer rather than a librarian. Reading his description should update that belief, but not replace it entirely.

## Why this reference is here

This lesson opens with a textbook example of the vivid-anecdote hook: the $700M Bayesian treasure hunt. Notice how tightly Grant controls the anecdote — one sentence of setup, one dollar figure, and an immediate return to the thesis ("So it's a formula worth understanding"). The anecdote is not there to entertain; it's there to answer the "why should I care" question in a single beat so the rest of the lesson doesn't have to justify itself. Concept notes that need to motivate a dry-looking formula should steal this exact shape.

The distinctive passage is a clean instance of Pattern 5 — the mantra close. "New evidence should update prior beliefs, not determine them" is the sentence the whole lesson is arranged to earn. Grant drops it early, in a concrete form ("update that belief, but not replace it entirely"), so that when it recurs in abstract form later the reader has already felt it. The mantra works because it survives being stripped of context: it is still true, and still non-obvious, when quoted on its own.

## Voice observations

- Opens with stakes (scientific discovery, ML, a specific sunken-ship treasure) before any math, but keeps the stakes one paragraph long — motivation, not marketing.
- Math/prose integration runs through concrete ratios ("20-to-1", "40 out of 100") rather than percentages or symbols, because the lesson's point is that intuition fires on counts.
- Reader address is collective ("our goal is for you") and goal-setting, which establishes that the lesson has a destination and the reader and author are walking there together.

## Global 3Blue1Brown caveats

- Prefer opening paragraphs and verbal definitions over mid-lesson "as you can see" moments that reference animations.
- Calibrate second-person address DOWN a notch — 3B1B uses more "you" than crypto concept notes should.
